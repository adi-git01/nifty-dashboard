## Valuation: why it looked unpredictable
Measured on all 943 valued stocks (6 Nov prices):
- **A 10% price move shifts the percentile by about 11 points** (median), so ratings flip near boundaries.
- **41% fail a gate.** Gate B (distribution mass vs scenario probabilities, +/-10 pp) fails for 33%. Failures go
  both ways (37 above, 23 below in a 192-stock sample) and track asymmetric scenarios (bear multiple far
  below base, bull capped near it), not data errors. The gate is left as is; the table already shows the
  rating with a quality icon rather than hiding it.
- **Assumptions the data cannot settle move the answer.** Peer multiples come from few, scattered peers;
  3-year, 5-year and last-12-month growth often disagree.

### Percentile band
Each run changes one assumption by an amount the stock's own evidence supports; the band is the range of
CMP percentiles across the runs.
- hurdle +/-1 pp; the other rho; own/peer anchor weight 65/35 and 35/65
- peer-median multiple +/- its standard error (1.25 x robust sd / sqrt(n))
- base growth = lowest / highest of 3y CAGR, 5y CAGR, TTM growth

Result: median width 16 points; 32% firm (<= 10), 44% soft (<= 25), 23% wide. **60% of valued stocks span
more than one rating**, now shown as e.g. "BUY-ACCUMULATE" with "P13-22". The snapshot stores the band as
two fair-value factors, so the table recomputes it at today's price (within 0.6 points of a full re-run).
The combined technical x valuation signal still uses the point rating; changing that needs a backtest.
